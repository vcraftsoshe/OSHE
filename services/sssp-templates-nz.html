What is an SSSP?

An SSSP is a site specific safety plan. It sets out how health and safety will be managed on one site or job. That means the risks, the controls, who is responsible, the emergency plan, and how everyone on site works together.

Most SSSPs get copied from the last job. Half the details are wrong and the plan sits in a drawer. OSHE fixes that by building the plan from the records you keep anyway.

How an SSSP gets built in OSHE

  1. Create the job. Add the site address, site contact, and start and finish dates.
  2. Fill in the SSSP template on the job. Every job starts from the same template, so nothing gets missed.
  3. Add the extras: a site map, emergency phone numbers, and any PDF appendices you need attached.
  4. Pick what applies to this job from your registers: plant and equipment, emergency procedures, hazardous substances, training and people.
  5. Link your task analysis templates and list the contractors on the job. Tick off each contractor's SSSP when you receive it.
  6. Hit generate. OSHE builds one PDF with your logo on it.

What goes into the PDF

The site

Site information, site map, site methodology and sign offs.

The risks

Task analyses and task analysis templates for the job, plant and equipment, and hazardous substances.

The people

Contractor list, plus a training and competency register for the people on the job.

When things go wrong

Emergency procedures, notifiable event and notifiable works procedures, and an incident register.

It keeps up with the job

Jobs change. When you regenerate the SSSP, OSHE pulls in the latest toolbox talks, site reviews and incident notifications logged against the job, for the number of days you set. Older versions stay on the job, so you can show what the plan said and when.

Your crew can open the latest SSSP from the OSHE mobile app whenever they have signal.

SSSP questions, answered

What is an SSSP in New Zealand?

An SSSP (site specific safety plan) is a document that sets out how health and safety will be managed on a particular site or job. It covers the risks, the controls, who is responsible, emergency plans, and how the businesses on site work together.

Is an SSSP a legal requirement in NZ?

The Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 (HSWA) does not name SSSPs. It does require every PCBU to manage risks so far as is reasonably practicable, and to consult, co-operate and co-ordinate with other PCBUs when their duties overlap (section 34). An SSSP is a common way to show you have done that, and many principals and main contractors ask for one before you start work.

What should an SSSP include?

A good SSSP covers the site and job details, who is responsible for health and safety, the hazards and controls for the work, hazardous substances, plant and equipment, emergency procedures, training and competency, incident reporting, how contractors are managed, and sign off.

Does OSHE give me an SSSP template?

Yes. Every job in OSHE starts from the same SSSP template. You fill in the site details, choose which registers, people and task analyses apply, and OSHE builds one PDF with your logo.

Can I update the SSSP during the job?

Yes. Regenerate it whenever things change. OSHE pulls in the latest toolbox talks, site reviews and incident notifications for the job, and keeps earlier versions on the job.

How do I track contractors' SSSPs?

List each contractor on the job in OSHE and tick when you have received their SSSP. The contractor list also goes into your own SSSP PDF.

Can workers see the SSSP on site?

Yes. The latest SSSP can be downloaded from the OSHE mobile app when the phone has an internet connection.
